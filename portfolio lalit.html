Stock market analysis

Exploratory analysis of four years (2021 to 2024) of daily prices for 10 tickers across 7 sectors, 10,430 rows in all. It measures returns, moving averages, volatility, correlation and drawdown to compare sectors on risk as well as reward.

  • Python
  • Pandas
  • NumPy
  • Matplotlib
  • Seaborn
  • Time series

The prices are simulated with geometric Brownian motion, with a different drift and volatility for each sector. The method is the real part. The market data is not.

Price trends. Daily closing prices for all 10 tickers, 2021 to 2024. ENRG ends the period far below where it started.

Moving averages. TCHX with 20-day and 50-day moving averages. When the 20-day line crosses the 50-day line, the short-term trend has changed.

Correlation. Correlation of daily returns for every pair of tickers. Every value sits between −0.06 and +0.06, because each price path is simulated on its own.

Volatility. 20-day rolling volatility, annualised. The Technology tickers run highest and UTIL runs lowest.

Sector returns. Average total return by sector: Consumer +49.0%, Financials +38.3%, Healthcare +22.6%, Utilities +14.9%, Technology +6.5%, Industrials −15.8%, Energy −65.5%.

Risk vs return. Annual volatility against annual return for each ticker, coloured by sector. HLTZ, FINC and CONS earned the highest annual returns, at 12.1%, 11.4% and 9.7%.

Drawdown. Peak-to-trough decline for TCHY over time. Its maximum drawdown is −52.9%.

What the data showed

  • FINC, CONS and HLTZ had the best risk-adjusted returns, with Sharpe ratios of 0.42, 0.40 and 0.37.
  • ENRG was the weakest. It fell 65.5% and suffered the deepest drawdown at 71.3%.
  • Technology was the most volatile sector (TCHY at 41.1% and TCHX at 33.7% a year). UTIL was the calmest at 11.7%.
  • Daily-return correlations all sit between −0.06 and +0.06. That is expected for independently simulated prices. Real market data would show clusters by sector.

How the numbers are calculated

  • Daily returns are log returns, ln(close today / close yesterday).
  • Annual return is the mean daily return × 252. Annual volatility is the daily standard deviation × √252.
  • Sharpe ratio is (annual return − 3% risk-free rate) ÷ annual volatility.
  • Max drawdown is the largest fall from a running peak to a later low.

What it automates

  • Follow-up status turns OVERDUE, DUE TODAY or UPCOMING from the next follow-up date.
  • Lead score is 100 for Won, 0 for Lost, and 80, 60 or 40 for High, Medium or Low priority.
  • The dashboard reports total, open and won leads, conversion, overdue and due-today follow-ups, pipeline value and weighted pipeline, with two charts.
  • An optional Apps Script stamps Last Contact when a status changes and suggests a follow-up two days after each contact.

Formulas behind the calculated columns

// Follow-up Status (N5)
=IF(L5="","",IF(L5<TODAY(),"OVERDUE",
  IF(L5=TODAY(),"DUE TODAY","UPCOMING")))

// Lead Score (O5)
=IF(I5="","",IF(I5="Won",100,IF(I5="Lost",0,
  IF(J5="High",80,IF(J5="Medium",60,40)))))

// Weighted Value (S5)
=IF(P5="","",P5*IF(R5="",0,R5)/100)

An example of the query pattern I practise: filter, join, group, total

-- Revenue by region and category, delivered orders only
SELECT c.region,
       p.category,
       COUNT(*) AS orders,
       SUM(o.quantity * o.unit_price * (1 - o.discount)) AS revenue
FROM orders o
JOIN customers c ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
JOIN products  p ON p.product_id  = o.product_id
WHERE o.status = 'Delivered'
GROUP BY c.region, p.category
ORDER BY revenue DESC;
